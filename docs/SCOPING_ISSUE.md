## Research finding

The hypothesis is substantially supported for a bounded workflow: structured financial content can be purchased through provider MCP/API/feed offerings and used with our own models, orchestration and UI. Buying content removes much collection/normalization work. Financial semantics, deterministic methods, source support, editable outputs, information barriers and bank acceptance remain substantial.

Public FactSet catalog inspection found company screening, fundamentals, estimates, debt/bond and private-market tools, while its M&A tool description specifies publicly traded targets. S&P/Kensho, LSEG, Daloopa and PitchBook are alternatives/complements, not interchangeable subscriptions. Actual contracted schemas, data quality, rights, prices and SLAs remain unverified.

## One-month challenge

One sector, one subject plus 5–10 listed peers, one provider, historical/LTM comps, bounded scenario DCF, source-linked corporate briefing, fixed editable PPTX/XLSX exports, review states and frozen snapshots. Use public/synthetic or expressly permitted data; no bank-production claim.

One full-time owner with an Opus 5.5 lead and bounded Sonnet/Haiku-class workers on **one Max 20× subscription**. Month-one owner effort low/base/high: **101/128/192 hours**. Base capacity is 160 hours over 20 working days, including 32 hours reserved for rework/interruptions/usage waits. Additional specialist review is roughly 12–16 finance hours and 8–12 security/platform hours, plus provider/procurement capacity. If the owner must absorb those roles, cut scope or extend time.

Illustrative kickoff **26 October 2026**, stretch demonstration **20 November 2026**; neither is an authorized start or commitment. A 6–8-week fallback is appropriate if access/semantics/review consume contingency. Defer DCF first while retaining complete, sourced comps and reviewed outputs.
